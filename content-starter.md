## Personal Injury
**Slug:** `injury`
**Hero Headline:** Injured?
**Hero Subhead:** If someone else's negligence left you hurt, you deserve a lawyer who fights for what your case is actually worth — not just the fastest settlement.

**Body:**
Personal injury covers situations where you were hurt because of someone
else's carelessness or recklessness — car accidents, slip and falls,
dog bites, defective products, and more. The challenge most people run into
isn't whether they have a case; it's finding a lawyer who will actually
fight for full value rather than pushing a quick settlement that benefits
the firm more than the client.

Insurance companies are sophisticated negotiators with one goal: pay out as
little as possible. Without an experienced attorney, most people settle for
far less than their injury is actually worth — often before they even know
the full extent of their medical needs.

We match people with attorneys who specialize in the specific type of
injury involved, have a track record of trial-readiness (not just quick
settlements), and take cases on contingency, meaning you pay nothing unless
they win your case.

**FAQs:**
- Q: Do I have to pay anything upfront? / A: No. Personal injury attorneys typically work on contingency, meaning they only get paid if you win, usually as a percentage of the settlement or verdict.
- Q: How long do I have to file a claim? / A: This varies significantly by state and case type — it can range from one to several years. Don't wait to find out; deadlines can bar your claim entirely.
- Q: What if the accident was partly my fault? / A: Many states still allow you to recover damages even if you were partially at fault, though it can reduce the amount. An attorney can explain how this applies in your state.

## Employment
**Slug:** `employment`
**Hero Headline:** Employment Issue?
**Hero Subhead:** Wrongful termination, discrimination, unpaid wages, or harassment — employment cases require an attorney who knows how to go up against a company's legal team.

**Body:**
Employment law covers a wide range of workplace wrongs: wrongful
termination, discrimination based on race, gender, age, disability, or
other protected status, sexual harassment, retaliation for whistleblowing,
and unpaid wages or overtime violations.

These cases are often intimidating because you're going up against a
company that has in-house counsel or an outside law firm on retainer.
Many people assume they have no chance and don't pursue a claim they'd
otherwise win.

We connect people with employment attorneys who take these cases
seriously and, in most cases involving employer misconduct, offer
contingency-based representation — meaning you don't pay unless they
recover something for you.

**FAQs:**
- Q: Can I be fired for reporting harassment? / A: No — retaliation for reporting harassment or discrimination is generally illegal, and retaliation itself can become a separate claim.
- Q: How do I know if I was wrongfully terminated? / A: Most employment is "at-will," meaning you can generally be fired for any legal reason. It becomes wrongful when the real reason is discrimination, retaliation, or violates a contract or public policy.
- Q: Is there a deadline to file a discrimination claim? / A: Yes — federal discrimination claims often must be filed with the EEOC within 180-300 days of the incident, so don't wait to get advice.

## Medical Malpractice
**Slug:** `medical-malpractice`
**Hero Headline:** Medical Malpractice?
**Hero Subhead:** When a medical professional's negligence causes harm, you need an attorney with real medical-case experience — these are some of the hardest cases to win without the right expertise.

**Body:**
Medical malpractice occurs when a healthcare provider's negligence — a
missed diagnosis, surgical error, medication mistake, or failure to warn of
risks — causes real harm. These cases are notoriously difficult to win
because they require medical expert testimony and lawyers who understand
both the law and the underlying medicine.

Because of this complexity, many general-practice injury lawyers won't
take these cases, or don't have the resources to properly litigate them
against hospital systems with substantial legal defense budgets.

We focus on connecting people with attorneys who specifically handle
medical malpractice, have relationships with credible medical experts, and
have the resources to take a case the distance if needed.

**FAQs:**
- Q: How do I know if what happened to me was malpractice versus just a bad outcome? / A: Not every bad medical outcome is malpractice — it has to be shown that the provider deviated from the accepted standard of care. An attorney can review your medical records to assess this.
- Q: How long do medical malpractice cases take? / A: These cases often take longer than typical injury claims, sometimes one to three years or more, due to the need for expert review and the complexity of proving negligence.
- Q: Do I need my medical records before contacting a lawyer? / A: It helps, but it's not required to start. An attorney can help you request the relevant records once they take your case.

## Pharmaceutical & Medical Device
**Slug:** `pharmaceutical-medical-device`
**Hero Headline:** Harmed by a Drug or Medical Device?
**Hero Subhead:** Dangerous drugs and defective medical devices often lead to large multi-district litigation — you need a lawyer connected to the right case network.

**Body:**
When a medication or medical device causes harm due to a manufacturing
defect, inadequate warnings, or a design flaw the manufacturer knew about,
you may have a claim against the manufacturer — separate from any claim
against a treating doctor.

These cases frequently become part of larger multi-district litigation
(MDL) or class actions when many people are harmed by the same product.
Being connected with an attorney who's already involved in the relevant
litigation can significantly affect the outcome and efficiency of your
case.

We help identify whether your situation fits an existing case network and
connect you with attorneys actively litigating claims involving the
specific drug or device involved.

**FAQs:**
- Q: What's the difference between an MDL and a class action? / A: In a class action, claims are combined into one case with one outcome for everyone. In an MDL, cases stay individual but are coordinated for pretrial proceedings, which often results in different settlement amounts per person based on their specific harm.
- Q: Is it too late if the drug or device has already been recalled? / A: Not necessarily. A recall can actually support your claim, but you should still act promptly due to filing deadlines.
- Q: Do I need to have stopped using the product to file a claim? / A: No, but you should talk to your doctor about your treatment before making any changes.

## Criminal Defense
**Slug:** `criminal-defense`
**Hero Headline:** Facing Criminal Charges?
**Hero Subhead:** Referral available in your area. Unlike our free-referral practice areas, criminal defense attorneys charge for their services — but finding the right one still matters enormously.

**Body:**
Facing criminal charges is one of the most stressful situations a person
can go through, and the quality of your legal representation can shape the
rest of your life. Unlike the free-referral practice areas on this site,
criminal defense attorneys charge for their services — but we can still
help you find someone reputable in your area rather than whoever has the
biggest billboard.

Public defenders provide a constitutional right to representation, but
they're often managing extremely high caseloads. If you have any ability
to retain private counsel, having an attorney who can dedicate real time to
your case can make a meaningful difference.

**FAQs:**
- Q: Can I afford a private criminal defense attorney? / A: Costs vary widely by case complexity and location. Many defense attorneys offer payment plans or flat fees for certain charge types — ask directly during a consultation.
- Q: Should I talk to police before getting a lawyer? / A: Generally, it's advisable to consult an attorney before answering questions beyond identifying yourself. You have the right to remain silent and the right to an attorney.

## Family Law
**Slug:** `family-law`
**Hero Headline:** Family Law Matter?
**Hero Subhead:** Referral available in your area. Divorce, custody, and support cases benefit enormously from the right attorney — we can help you find one.

**Body:**
Divorce, child custody, child support, and other family law matters are
deeply personal and often emotionally difficult. As with criminal defense,
family law attorneys charge for their services, but we can still connect
you with a reputable attorney in your area rather than leaving you to
guess based on advertising.

The right family law attorney can make an enormous difference in outcomes
around custody arrangements, support calculations, and property division —
particularly in contested cases.

**FAQs:**
- Q: Do I need a lawyer for an uncontested divorce? / A: Not always, but even in amicable situations, a lawyer can make sure paperwork is filed correctly and your interests are protected in the final agreement.
- Q: How is child custody decided? / A: Courts generally decide based on the "best interest of the child" standard, which considers factors like each parent's living situation, involvement, and ability to provide stability. An attorney can help you understand how this applies in your state.
